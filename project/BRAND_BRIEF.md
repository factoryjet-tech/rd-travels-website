# RD TRAVELS — BRAND BRIEF
## What this business actually is, and what to never get wrong

> **Read this before opening Claude Design. Read this before writing any prompt. The earlier attempt failed because the designer didn't understand the business.**

---

## The business model in one paragraph

RD Travels is an Indian "travels" agency in Ahmedabad. Customers call, WhatsApp, or fill a form requesting a vehicle for a trip. RD Travels dispatches a vehicle **with a professional driver** to pick the customer up at the requested time and place, and drive them to their destination — whether that's a 30-minute airport drop or a 5-day Rajasthan tour. The customer never drives. They are a passenger. The driver is a trained, employed RD Travels staff member.

This is the standard Indian model for what Indians simply call "**travels**" (the noun is plural by convention — "I'll book a travels"). It is fundamentally different from:

- **Self-drive rental** (Zoomcar, Revv) — customer drives
- **Cab aggregators** (Uber, Ola) — short urban rides on app
- **Bus ticketing** (RedBus) — single seats on someone else's bus
- **Luxury travel agencies** (Black Tomato, Trufflepig) — curated international experiences

RD Travels is none of those. It is a **vehicle-with-driver rental operator** for outstation, long-distance, group, and special-occasion travel within India.

## The brand's positioning (one line)

> **RD Travels — Ahmedabad's direct travels operator since 2015. Cars, Tempo Travellers, and buses with professional drivers. Owned fleet, transparent prices, no brokers.**

### What this means for design

1. **"Direct" is the differentiator.** Every visible word, button, and section should reinforce that customers are talking to the operator, not a middleman. Brokers and aggregators add markup, lose phone messages, blame the operator when things go wrong. RD Travels doesn't.

2. **"With driver" must be visible.** Every vehicle mention must communicate that a trained driver is included. This is a feature, not an afterthought. (Many Western design references won't show this — a Turo or Sixt page emphasizes the vehicle alone. RD Travels must show vehicle AND driver as a single offering.)

3. **"All vehicle classes, one operator" is the second differentiator.** A wedding family doesn't want to call three vendors for the bridal car, the band's bus, and the extended family Tempos. RD Travels does all three. The vehicle showcase must communicate this breadth.

4. **Transparent pricing is the third differentiator.** Indian travels customers are used to opaque pricing — broker markups, surprise toll charges, last-minute "convenience fees." RD Travels publishes per-km rates and 8-hour package rates. The site must lead with these numbers.

5. **Ten years (since 2015) is real.** Don't bury it. A 10-year track record in the Indian travels industry — which has high churn — is meaningful trust.

## Voice and tone

### How RD Travels speaks
- **Direct.** "Book direct. Get a quote in 60 seconds." Not "Embark on a journey of seamless travel."
- **Specific.** "₹11/km outstation" — not "competitive rates."
- **Honest.** "No advance to enquire" — not "free quote with no obligation."
- **Hospitable.** Indian hospitality is warm but not effusive. Confident, not desperate. Welcoming, not gushing.
- **Knowledgeable.** "We've run this route for ten years — we know which dhaba is open at 4am on NH 48." Specificity that proves operator-level expertise.

### Forbidden vocabulary
- ❌ "Amazing" / "incredible" / "mind-blowing"
- ❌ "Luxurious experience" / "luxury travel" (unless describing a specific Volvo)
- ❌ "World-class" / "best-in-class" / "premium" (used as noun)
- ❌ "Embark on a journey" / "discover" / "explore"
- ❌ "Seamless" / "frictionless" / "effortless"
- ❌ "Trusted by thousands" without a real number
- ❌ Exclamation marks in body copy

### Approved vocabulary
- ✅ "Direct from the operator"
- ✅ "Owned fleet, employed drivers" (or "Owned fleet, trained drivers")
- ✅ "GST-itemised quote" / "GST-clear pricing"
- ✅ "Tourist Permit + AITP" (industry credentials, real)
- ✅ "Since 2015"
- ✅ "Pan-India" (for routing)
- ✅ "Pickup" / "drop" / "outstation" / "round trip" (industry terms Indian customers use)
- ✅ "WhatsApp us" / "Call us" (direct, action-oriented)

## What to avoid in design (anti-patterns)

### ❌ Editorial luxury magazine
The previous attempt tried this. It looked like Mr & Mrs Smith. It was rejected. Italic serif flourishes, hairline rules, drop caps, and ivory backgrounds signal "boutique hotel chain in Bali," not "reliable travels desk in Ahmedabad." This is the most important anti-pattern.

### ❌ Aggregator UI
MakeMyTrip, Yatra, Goibibo. Yellow CTAs, dense filter rails, "999 happy customers" counters, tiny fonts on dense info, modal popups. This is what RD Travels is competing AGAINST. The visual must look distinctly like a direct operator, not a marketplace.

### ❌ AI startup design
Inter typeface, purple gradients, glassmorphism, every section a card with rounded-2xl shadow, "Book your dream ride" hero copy, abstract decorative blobs. Generic and forgettable.

### ❌ Cheap luxury
Gold-on-black gradients, faux-script fonts, lens-flare photography, the Wedding Wire aesthetic. Trying to look premium and missing.

### ❌ Stock travel photography
Generic "couple looking at sunset," "happy family in car," "highway aerial drone shot." These signal Shutterstock, not Ahmedabad. Use real fleet photos when available; until then, prefer typographic compositions or stylized vehicle illustrations.

## What to embrace (positive direction)

### ✅ White-dominant, saffron-accented
70% white surfaces, 30% saffron accents. Clean, hospitable, distinctly Indian. The saffron isn't just a brand color — it's a cultural signal (the spice, the flag, the marigold, the temple). It's the most efficient way to look Indian without looking touristy.

### ✅ Single-family typography (Plus Jakarta Sans)
A single typeface family that handles everything from 80px display headlines to 12px form labels, using weight contrast (200–800) for hierarchy. Plus Jakarta Sans was designed in Indonesia for the Jakarta government — it has South Asian DNA, professional warmth, and exceptional tabular numerals for pricing tables. It feels welcoming and professional simultaneously, which is exactly the brand's voice.

### ✅ Vehicle-forward visual language
Show the vehicles. Show seat counts. Show the per-km rates. Show a small driver-included indicator. Customers booking transport want to see what's actually being offered, not poetic travel writing.

### ✅ WhatsApp as a first-class CTA
Every page, every screen, sticky on mobile. WhatsApp is how 80%+ of Indian travels bookings happen. The button is green (WhatsApp brand green is fine — it adds recognition), prominent, and pre-fills a useful message.

### ✅ Pricing transparency as design feature
Don't hide rates behind "Get quote." Publish them. The pricing table is one of the strongest trust signals on the site. Use Plus Jakarta Sans's tabular numerals so prices align cleanly.

### ✅ Specific, real trust signals
"Since 2015 · GST registered · Tourist Permit + AITP · Pan-India · 24×7 active-trip support" — used as a footer strip on every page. These are real, verifiable, and they outperform generic "trusted" badges 10:1.

## A simple test for any design decision

Ask: *"Would Ronak Dineshbhai Barot, the founder of a 10-year-old travels operator in Ahmedabad, look at this and feel like it represents his business?"*

- If it looks like a Bali resort → too editorial, reject.
- If it looks like Goibibo → too aggregator, reject.
- If it looks like a Y Combinator startup → too generic-tech, reject.
- If it looks like the kind of place a wedding family in Satellite (an Ahmedabad neighborhood) would trust to send their bridal car → accept.

That's the brief.
